You stay hidden. The thundering of the horses grows louder as the carriage 
nears, then the hoofbeats grow less frantic. You hold your breath as the 
carriage rolls to a stop not far from you.

Carefully, you peer around the tree as two people dismount. The first is a 
woman in a white dress with elbow-length gloves and a feathered fan. Behind 
her, the second person paints a stark contrast, dressed head-to-toe in red 
military wear.

"Isn't it just like those tree-loving hermits to hole up leagues away from the 
capital?" The woman says, waving her fan languidly in front of her. "Is it 
quite far still, Graves?"

"No, your majesty," says the stern-looking guard.

"Good, the sooner we make sure the crown's rule is safe, the sooner we can 
return to civilized society."

The crunching of wheels against the path signals the arrival of a second 
carriage. The people who descend from this one are much less appointed than 
the first pair. An older man in rough hewn robes carries a book in his hands, 
while a younger man trails in his wake. 

The two pairs come face to face, caught in a silent stalemate before the woman 
in white breaks the silence. 

"Well," she says, cocking a delicate eyebrow. "Hand the book over."

The older man's hands tighten reflexively around the leatherbound cover. "This 
ledger is my life's work, your majesty," the man starts. "To that end, I must 
ask what you intend to do with it."

"What else?" The royal scoffs. "I'm going to burn the bloody thing before it 
has the chance to sink this kingdom into chaos."

The older man makes the slightest attempt to back away before the soldier 
swoops forward and pries the book from his hands. The woman smiles coldly at 
the bewildered pair.

"This is for the best, you'll see. Now that the Order of the Oak has crumbled 
under their own sanctimonious weight, the people need to have trust in their 
leaders."
